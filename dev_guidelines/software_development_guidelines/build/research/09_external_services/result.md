# Research result: 09_external_services (area EXT)

## L1 headline

Best practice for consuming and choosing external services is well grounded on the technical side (call safety, deprecation signalling, supply-chain hygiene of CI services, licence mechanics) and thin on the commercial side (SLA terms, pricing, vendor assessment), where regulation (EU Data Act, GDPR Art. 28) and vendor documents supply most of the T1 text; certainty is high for the consume-safely and licence clusters, medium for contract, portability and vendor assessment, low for general dev-tool and hosting evaluation (no T1/T2 source found).

Pinned:
- Sources by tier: T1 = 22, T2 = 11, T3 = 4, T4 = 2 (39 sources; the 2 T4 are a documented debate and unread search summaries, neither is the sole ground of a claim).
- Claims: 51 (EXT-C01 to EXT-C51).
- Contested topics: (1) abstraction layer and multi-cloud versus accepting lock-in; (2) never-break evolution versus versioned breaking changes; (3) tolerant reader versus strict validation of third-party responses; (4) circuit breaker versus retry budget alone; (5) copyleft and network-copyleft allowed versus banned; (6) depending on single-vendor-controlled open source versus accepting relicensing risk; (7) vetting depth of a vendor scaled to criticality versus uniform checklist (inferred).
- Failed fetches on T1 sources: EUR-Lex Regulation (EU) 2023/2854 (Data Act) and EUR-Lex Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 (DORA) both returned no text (Data Act read via the European Commission explainer page instead; DORA only via search-result summaries, not read); gnu.org license-list.html (connection reset; GPL FAQ and ASF page used instead); AICPA SOC 2 page returned no criteria text.

## L2 claims

Clusters (agreement; certainty; ids):
1. A remote call to an external service needs a timeout, a bounded retry policy with backoff and jitter at one layer only, and retries only for idempotent or keyed operations. consensus; grounded; C01 to C05.
2. Protect the caller from a failing dependency: deadlines, retry budget or circuit breaker, load shedding, graceful degradation with a tested fallback. majority (breaker versus budget contested); grounded; C06 to C09.
3. Treat every third-party response as untrusted input: TLS, validation, bounded resources, redirect allowlist; but accept unknown fields. contested (tolerant versus strict); grounded; C10 to C12.
4. Isolate the service behind your own boundary and configuration: attached resource, translation layer, no vendor model in your domain. majority; grounded; C13 to C15.
5. An SLA is a commercial remedy with exclusions and a claim burden, not an availability guarantee; plan from SLOs and measured behaviour. consensus; grounded; C16 to C20.
6. Deprecation must be signalled, dated and observable; consumers must watch for it; providers differ between never-break and versioned evolution. majority/contested; grounded; C21 to C26.
7. Lock-in is a spectrum to price; portability is a contract and data-format property to verify at selection, increasingly a legal right (EU). majority; grounded (regulation via secondary Commission page); C27 to C32.
8. Pricing and licence terms of a dependency can change unilaterally; treat them as a risk with owner and tripwire. contested; grounded for the events, inferred for the rule; C33 to C35.
9. Assess a vendor's security and supply-chain posture before depending on it, and contract for it; depth scaled to criticality. majority; grounded for criteria, inferred for scaling; C36 to C38.
10. CI and hosting services are part of your supply chain: pin third-party actions, least privilege, hosted isolated builds, no self-hosted runners for untrusted code. consensus; grounded; C39 to C42.
11. Licence obligations depend on the licence family, the distribution model (ship versus host) and the combination; record them machine-readably and run a compliance process. majority (policy contested); grounded; C43 to C50.
12. General evaluation of dev tools and platforms (non-library) has no T1/T2 framework found. open; C51.

Live debates (forces in tension):
- Abstraction and multi-cloud versus accepting lock-in: exit risk and reversibility against cost of ownership, cognitive load, underused features (C14, C28, C29).
- Never-break versus versioned evolution: consumer stability against provider speed and simplicity of the foundation (C23, C24).
- Tolerant reader versus strict validation: evolution without breakage against security and detection of drift (C10, C11).
- Circuit breaker versus retry budget: protection and fast failure against complexity and false opens (C07).
- Retry versus single-point-of-fault amplification: success rate for the caller against load on the provider (C02).
- Copyleft and AGPL allowed versus banned: delivery speed and reuse against control of own source (C46, C47, C48).
- Single-vendor-controlled open source versus relicensing risk: speed and features against exit risk (C34).
- Vendor vetting depth: security and exit risk against delivery speed (C37).

Candidate central decisions of the area (others depend on them):
- EXT-D1: What must an external service provide before it may be a hard dependency: availability target, SLA remedy that matches the loss, deprecation notice, data export format, exit path (C16, C21, C27, C30).
- EXT-D2: Boundary rule: where retries, timeouts, validation and the vendor-model translation live, and which team owns them (C01, C07, C10, C13, C14).
- EXT-D3: Licence policy by distribution model: which families are allowed, notice-only, review-needed or banned, and who decides (C46 to C49).
- EXT-D4: Vendor assessment depth by criticality, and what evidence counts (C36, C37).
- EXT-D5: Trust rule for CI and hosting services: pin, least privilege, isolation level (C39 to C42).

Not covered: SaaS contract clauses beyond SLA and GDPR Art. 28 (indemnity, liability caps, source-code escrow, price-increase caps); SOC 2 / ISO 27001 report content (page yielded no criteria text); DORA and Cyber Resilience Act primary text; Retry-After semantics (RFC 9110 section 10.2.3 not returned in full); rate-limit design from a provider's side; webhook consumption (signature checks, replay, ordering); API-key and OAuth credential lifecycle for third-party APIs; general evaluation criteria for dev tools, hosting and platform choice (no T1/T2 found); data residency and sovereignty depth; SBOM/VEX for vendors; open-core and fair-source licence taxonomy beyond BSL; patent clauses beyond the ASF note.

### Claims

**EXT-C01** | claim: Set a connection timeout and a request timeout on every remote call, sized from the dependency's measured latency percentile and an accepted false-timeout rate. | level: practice | stack: neutral | sources: S34 (Timeouts), S24 (Deadlines), S13 (list of vulnerable behaviours: no timeouts) | agreement: consensus | certainty: grounded

**EXT-C02** | claim: Retry only transient failures, with capped exponential backoff and jitter, a per-request attempt cap, and retries at one layer of the stack only; do not retry permanent or malformed-request errors. | level: practice | stack: neutral | sources: S34 (Retries and backoff, Jitter), S24 (Retries: randomized exponential backoff, limiting retries, retry amplification, response-code differentiation), S28 (retryable versus non-retryable codes) | agreement: consensus | certainty: grounded. Note: S34 states retries are "selfish": the caller buys success with the provider's capacity, the force in tension being caller success rate against provider load.

**EXT-C03** | claim: Retry a non-read operation only when it is idempotent by contract or protected by an idempotency key; treat creation without a key as unsafe to retry. | level: practice | stack: neutral | sources: S03 (9.2.2 definition of idempotent), S34 (Idempotency), S29 (Idempotency-Key makes POST and PATCH fault-tolerant; draft is expired, so no standard yet), S28 (scope limited to requests where repeats do not change state) | agreement: consensus on principle, contested on mechanism (header not standardised) | certainty: grounded

**EXT-C04** | claim: Honour a provider's explicit throttling and wait signals (429, 503, Retry-After) rather than retrying blind; an overloaded dependency's error may justify an immediate longer pause. | level: practice | stack: neutral (HTTP) | sources: S04 (Accelerated circuit breaking, note on 429 and 503), S03 (Retry-After named as the timing field; section 10.2.3 detail not returned) | agreement: consensus | certainty: inferred (S04 grounded; S03 semantics not read in full)

**EXT-C05** | claim: Use the provider's well-tested client or SDK for timeout and retry handling rather than hand-written socket code, and verify what the SDK retries by default. | level: practice | stack: neutral | sources: S34 (Implementation considerations: well-tested client libraries), S21 (service guides recommend retries and Microsoft-provided SDKs) | agreement: majority | certainty: grounded

**EXT-C06** | claim: Bound the whole system's retry volume with a retry budget (process-wide cap or token bucket) so that retries cannot amplify an outage. | level: practice | stack: neutral | sources: S24 (Retry budgets: example of 60 per minute), S34 (Retry budgets; token bucket as a circuit-breaker alternative) | agreement: consensus | certainty: grounded

**EXT-C07** | claim: Choose between a circuit breaker and a retry budget (or both) per dependency; a breaker earns its complexity when a dependency fails slowly, is shared by many instances, or has long time-outs that would pin threads; it is unneeded where retry algorithms suffice, in message-driven designs with dead-letter queues, or where the platform or mesh already breaks circuits. | level: decision | stack: neutral | sources: S04 (When to use; might not be suitable), S32 (breaker needs fallback, state logging, operator trip and reset), S34 (token bucket as alternative) | agreement: contested | positions: (a) breaker with half-open probing and logged state changes, plus fallback (S32, S04; forces: protection of own resources, fast failure; wins for slow-failing synchronous dependencies with many callers). (c) neither: queue and dead-letter (S04; force: decoupling; wins in event-driven designs). | certainty: grounded

**EXT-C08** | claim: Propagate a deadline across calls, and check the remaining time before doing work; do not start work for a request whose caller has given up. | level: practice | stack: neutral | sources: S24 (Deadlines, deadline and cancellation propagation) | agreement: consensus (one T2 source, generally accepted) | certainty: grounded

**EXT-C09** | claim: Design a degraded mode for each non-essential external dependency (cached or default result, reduced feature) and exercise it regularly, since it rarely runs in steady state; turn hard dependencies into soft ones where possible. | level: principle | stack: neutral | sources: S24 (Graceful degradation: keep it simple, monitor and exercise it), S32 (clients must implement fallback), S04 (Exception handling; Example), round-1 SRC-C32 for the same stance | agreement: consensus | certainty: grounded | boundary: OPERATE

**EXT-C10** | claim: Treat every third-party response as untrusted input: validate and sanitize before use, use TLS, do not follow redirects blindly (keep an allowlist of redirect targets), and cap the resources spent on a response. | level: principle | stack: neutral | sources: S13 (Is the API vulnerable; How to prevent), S05 (input validation at the boundary) | agreement: consensus | certainty: grounded
